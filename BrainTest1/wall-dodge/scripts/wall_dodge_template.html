What you are looking at

A fly hovers in place. Walls fly at it, and each wall has its left or right side open. The fly can only steer sideways, and it is steered by a simulated piece of a real fly brain.

Eyes: the more wall a fly sees on one side, and the closer it gets, the harder that eye's looming neurons are driven.

Brain activity: 820 neurons cut out of the male fly connectome, drawn where they really sit. A dot flashes when the neuron fires; lines show its strongest connections lighting up.

Steering: if the right steering neurons (DNa) fire more than the left ones, the fly moves right, and the other way round.

Conditions: the controls swap or scramble the eyes, or use no brain at all, to test whether the real wiring is what makes the dodge work.

Assumption: we treat a DNa neuron as turning the fly toward its own side. We have not tested this, and the result depends on it. The eyes see a simple looming signal, not sonar. These are recorded simulation runs, not a live fly.
