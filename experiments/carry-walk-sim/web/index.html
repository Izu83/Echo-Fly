    How it works

    The fly walks forward at a steady pace. Only its brain decides where it turns.

    Eyes. Each eye looks from straight ahead to its side (the coloured rays). The more of an eye's view a wall fills, and the closer it is, the harder that eye's looming neurons (LPLC1, LPLC2, LPLC4) are driven, up to 150 spikes/s each.

    Brain. 416 LPLC neurons, 400 relay neurons and the 4 turning neurons DNa01/DNa02, joined by their real connections from the male CNS connectome (Janelia FlyEM). Every neuron is a leaky integrate-and-fire unit simulated every millisecond. Nothing about "turn away from walls" is written in the code: that comes from the wiring.

    Turning. Turning speed = gain × (right DNa rate − left DNa rate).

    Dopamine learning. The fly senses which direction its current target is (8 direction cells) and how close walls are (6 wall cells). Those cells reach the turning neurons through synapses that start out random, so a new fly has no idea where to go. PAM dopamine neurons fire when things go better than expected (getting closer to the target, a wall moving away, grabbing the load, arriving). PPL1 dopamine neurons fire when they go worse (bumping, a wall looming up, walking away). Synapses that were just used get stronger after PAM and weaker after PPL1. What it learns stays between attempts.

    Fixed compass (for comparison). Instead of learning, a compass sends extra spikes to the turning neuron on the side of the target.

    Built-in wall avoidance. Switch it off in Settings to disconnect the eyes from the connectome's looming neurons: the fly then has to learn from punishment alone that walls are bad.

    Controls. Set Eye wiring to swapped to plug each eye into the wrong side of the brain: the fly then steers into walls.
